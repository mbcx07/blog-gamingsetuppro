One Setup to Rule Them All: Unifying PS5, Xbox, and Switch in 2026

2026-05-28 · Consoles · Tags: consoles, One, gamingsetuppro

The modern gaming landscape is a buffet of exclusives, and few of us are willing to pick just one dish. Owning a PlayStation 5, an Xbox Series X, and a Nintendo Switch is no longer a luxury—it's a lifestyle. But the moment you stack three consoles under your TV, chaos threatens to take over: a tangle of HDMI cables, a remote control that only works with one device, and the constant shuffle of swapping inputs. The solution isn't to build three separate stations; it's to craft a single, unified setup that treats each console as a component of a larger ecosystem. In this guide, we'll break down how to merge the power of Sony, Microsoft, and Nintendo into one seamless command center, covering everything from display selection to cross-platform peripherals, and even streaming—all while keeping your budget and future-proofing in mind.

The 2026 Console Landscape: Power, Price, and Exclusives

Before you start rearranging your desk, it's worth understanding what each console brings to the table in 2026. The PlayStation 5, now available in both standard and Pro variants, remains the king of cinematic single-player experiences. The PS5 Pro, with its boosted GPU and advanced ray tracing, targets 4K at 60–120 fps in titles like Spider-Man 2 and Horizon Forbidden West. The standard PS5, meanwhile, offers incredible value at $499, delivering near-identical performance in most cross-platform games. On the Microsoft side, the Xbox Series X is a brute-force machine, often matching or exceeding the PS5 in raw compute, but its real strength lies in Game Pass—a subscription service that gives you access to hundreds of titles for a monthly fee. The Series S, at $299, is a budget-friendly entry point, though it sacrifices resolution and disc support. And then there's the Nintendo Switch, which, even with the rumored Switch 2 on the horizon, remains the go-to for portable gaming and family-friendly exclusives like Zelda and Mario. Its hybrid nature means you can play on the TV or on the go, but its hardware is significantly less powerful than its rivals, capping out at 1080p on the big screen.

When it comes to price-to-performance, the PS5 and Xbox Series X are neck-and-neck, but your choice will likely be dictated by exclusives. If you're a fan of God of War or Final Fantasy, PlayStation is non-negotiable. If you want the best value in multiplayer and a library that grows monthly, Xbox Game Pass is a steal. The Switch, meanwhile, is a secondary console—a companion for travel and couch co-op. The key to a unified setup is recognizing that each console has a distinct role, and your hardware should accommodate all three without compromise.

Designing the Multi-Console Command Center

The heart of any multi-console setup is the display. For the PS5 and Xbox Series X, you'll want a 4K TV or monitor that supports HDMI 2.1, which enables 4K at 120Hz, Variable Refresh Rate (VRR), and Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM). These features are essential for smooth, responsive gameplay, especially in competitive shooters. The Switch, on the other hand, only outputs 1080p, so it doesn't need the same bandwidth—but it will still benefit from a low-latency display. A 43-inch OLED TV is the sweet spot for a living room setup, offering perfect blacks and vibrant colors that make both AAA titles and indie gems pop. If you're on a desk, a 32-inch 4K 144Hz monitor with HDMI 2.1 is a fantastic choice, though you'll need to ensure it has at least two HDMI 2.1 ports to avoid constant cable swapping.

To connect all three consoles to a single display, you have two main options: an HDMI switch or an AV receiver. A high-quality HDMI 2.1 switch, like the one from Zeskit or Monoprice, can handle multiple inputs and automatically switch to the active device. Look for one that supports 4K 120Hz and VRR to avoid bottlenecks. Alternatively, a modern AV receiver not only switches inputs but also routes audio to a surround sound system—a boon for immersive gaming. Just be prepared to spend a bit more for a receiver with HDMI 2.1 support. Cable management is another critical aspect: use Velcro ties and cable sleeves to keep everything tidy, and consider a power strip with surge protection to handle the combined load. Finally, think about ergonomics—place your consoles in a well-ventilated area, ideally on a shelf with open sides, to prevent overheating.

Cross-Platform Peripherals: Controllers, Headsets, and More

One of the biggest headaches of owning multiple consoles is the proliferation of controllers. But you don't need a separate pad for each system. The Xbox Elite Series 2 controller is a favorite among PC and Xbox players, and it also works with the PS5 via a third-party adapter like the Brook Wingman XB. Conversely, the DualSense Edge offers excellent haptics and back paddles, and it can be used on Xbox with a similar adapter. For a more budget-friendly approach, consider the 8BitDo Pro 2, which natively supports Switch, PC, and mobile, and can be configured for Xbox and PlayStation via Bluetooth. If you're a stickler for native features like adaptive triggers, you'll want to use the official controllers for each console, but for casual play, a universal pad is a lifesaver.

Headsets are another area where you can consolidate. Look for a wireless headset that supports simultaneous Bluetooth and 2.4GHz connections, like the SteelSeries Arctis Nova Pro Wireless. This allows you to connect to your PC and console at the same time, so you can hear game audio and Discord chat without switching. For console-specific chat, you'll need to use the console's native party system, but many headsets have a mixer that lets you balance game and voice audio. If you're streaming, a dedicated USB microphone is a better investment than a headset mic—the HyperX QuadCast or Shure MV7 are excellent choices that work with any console via a capture card or PC.

Streaming from Consoles: The Essential Gear

If you want to share your gameplay with the world, streaming from consoles requires a bit of extra hardware. The simplest route is to use the built-in streaming apps on PS5 and Xbox, which broadcast directly to Twitch or YouTube. However, these are limited in terms of overlays, alerts, and scene switching. For a professional-grade stream, you'll need a capture card like the Elgato HD60 X, which connects between your console and your display, passing the video through to your TV while sending a copy to your PC. The PC then runs OBS Studio, where you can add overlays, webcam feeds, and alerts. This setup also allows you to use a single PC for both gaming and streaming, provided your CPU has enough cores—a Ryzen 7 or Intel Core i7 is recommended.

For the Switch, streaming is a bit trickier because the console's dock outputs 1080p, but the HD60 X handles that fine. You'll also need a chat link cable to capture party audio, or you can use a headset with a separate mic. If you're serious about streaming, consider a dual-PC setup: one for gaming, one for encoding. But for most console streamers, a single PC with a capture card is more than sufficient. Don't forget about lighting—a ring light or softbox will make your webcam feed look professional. And invest in a good quality webcam, like the Logitech C920 or the Elgato Facecam, to keep your viewers engaged.

Budgeting Your Console Setup: From Entry to Enthusiast

Building a unified console setup doesn't have to break the bank. Let's break down three tiers. On a budget, you can get away with a 27-inch 1080p 144Hz monitor (around $200), a basic HDMI switch (under $30), and a universal controller like the 8BitDo Pro 2 ($50). This setup will work fine for the Switch and the Series S, but you'll miss out on 4K on the PS5 and Xbox Series X. For a mid-range setup, step up to a 43-inch 4K 60Hz TV with HDMI 2.0 (around $400) and a 4K HDMI switch that supports 60Hz (around $60). You'll get crisp visuals, but you'll sacrifice 120Hz and VRR. If you're an enthusiast, invest in a 48-inch OLED TV with HDMI 2.1 (around $1,200) and a high-end switch or AV receiver (around $300). Add a premium headset like the Arctis Nova Pro ($350) and a capture card for streaming ($200), and you're looking at a total of around $2,000—but the experience is night and day.

When allocating your budget, prioritize the display first, as it affects every console. Next, invest in a good HDMI switch to avoid constant cable swapping. Peripherals like controllers and headsets can be upgraded over time, but a solid foundation will serve you for years. Remember that the consoles themselves are the biggest expense, but they're already in your possession. The goal is to maximize their potential without redundancy.

Future-Proofing: What to Consider for Next-Gen

The gaming industry is always evolving, and your setup should be ready for what's next. HDMI 2.1 is the current standard, but keep an eye on HDMI 2.2, which promises even higher bandwidth for 8K and 240Hz. While no console currently supports 8K gaming, the PS5 Pro and Xbox Series X are capable of upscaling to 8K, so a display with HDMI 2.1 is a safe bet. VRR and ALLM are already standard on modern TVs, but ensure your switch or receiver passes these signals through without degradation. Another trend is cloud gaming—services like Xbox Cloud Gaming and PlayStation Plus Premium allow you to stream games to any device, which could eventually reduce the need for physical consoles. However, for now, local hardware still offers the best performance and reliability.

To future-proof your setup, choose a display with at least two HDMI 2.1 ports, so you can connect both the PS5 and Xbox Series X directly, and use a switch for the Switch and any other devices. Also, consider a modular cable management system that allows you to add or remove cables easily. And don't forget about firmware updates—keep your consoles and peripherals updated to ensure compatibility with new features. Finally, think about the next generation of consoles. The Switch 2 is rumored to support 4K, which would make it a more serious contender in your setup. By building a flexible infrastructure now, you'll be ready to integrate new hardware without a complete overhaul.

Conclusion

Unifying your PS5, Xbox Series X, and Switch into a single, cohesive setup is not only possible—it's the smartest way to enjoy the best of all worlds. By focusing on a high-quality display, a reliable HDMI switch, and cross-platform peripherals, you can eliminate the clutter and frustration of juggling multiple systems. Whether you're a casual player or a dedicated streamer, the principles outlined here will help you build a command center that's both functional and future-proof. Remember, the goal isn't to have the most expensive gear; it's to have a setup that lets you jump from Elden Ring to Halo to Mario Kart without missing a beat. So take a deep breath, untangle those cables, and embrace the unified console experience. Your gaming room—and your sanity—will thank you.
